A stack is a linear data structure that follows the LIFO principle, that is last in, first out. The element inserted last is removed first.
Push adds an element on top of the stack. Example: pushing 10 and then 20 onto an empty stack gives 20 on top.
Pop removes the top element. Example: after popping from [10, 20], 20 is removed and 10 is the new top.
Both push and pop take O(1) constant time because only the top pointer changes.
Applications: undo operation in editors, evaluating postfix expressions and function call management in recursion.
